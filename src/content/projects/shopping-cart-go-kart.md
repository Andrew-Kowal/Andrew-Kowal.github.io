## Overview

A go-kart built from a recycled shopping cart, including the steering, chassis, and braking system — built as a hands-on physics class project.

## The problem

The project's goal was to design and build a working go-kart that could also demonstrate real physics concepts in action — velocity and acceleration, forces, friction, torque — rather than just calculating them on paper.

## What I built

Welded a shopping cart's steel frame into a go-kart chassis, with a small gas engine mounted at the rear driving the rear axle through a chain and sprocket. Steering runs through a column connected to the front axle, and a hand-lever-actuated brake runs off a cable to a caliper. ATV-style knobby tires replaced the cart's original casters.

## Technical details

- Drivetrain: small gas engine, chain-and-sprocket final drive to a solid rear axle.
- Steering: steering wheel on a column linked to the front axle.
- Brakes: hand-lever-actuated, cable-driven caliper.
- Chassis: the shopping cart's welded steel frame, reused as the main structure.

## Results

Finished a working, rideable go-kart and presented the build alongside physics calculations — velocity/acceleration, forces, friction, torque, and impact — on a project poster, including data from a custom-built speedometer.
