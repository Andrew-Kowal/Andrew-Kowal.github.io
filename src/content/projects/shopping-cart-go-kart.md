## Overview

A go-kart built without starting from a kit chassis: a recycled shopping cart frame cut, reinforced, and welded into a working vehicle, with scratch-built Ackermann steering and hydraulic brakes. It also doubled as a hands-on physics class project.

## The problem

The goal was to adapt an imperfect, nonstandard structure — a shopping cart frame — into a functional vehicle: cutting and reshaping the frame, mounting an engine and drivetrain, and building the steering and braking systems from scratch, rather than starting from a kit chassis.

## What I built

Cut away the lower cart body with an angle grinder, keeping just the basket section as the base structure, then MIG welded steel tubing underneath to reinforce the frame and welded a square steel tubing mount at the rear to carry the engine. Installed the rear axle using a purchased axle kit and pillow block bearings, then mounted a Predator 212 6.5 HP engine and connected it to the axle with a centrifugal clutch and chain drive.

Built the steering system from scratch: fabricated the steering shaft and an Ackermann steering linkage — the triangulated geometry that keeps the inside and outside wheels turning at the correct angles — and installed it with mounting brackets, a bearing, tie rods, and a go-kart-style steering wheel. Installed a hydraulic brake on the rear axle, with the brake handle mounted on one side of the steering wheel, and adapted a bike brake lever as a mechanical throttle control on the other side.

## Technical details

- Chassis: shopping cart basket section, angle-ground free of the lower cart body, reinforced with MIG-welded steel tubing.
- Drivetrain: Predator 212 6.5 HP engine, centrifugal clutch, chain drive to a rear axle on pillow block bearings.
- Steering: scratch-built Ackermann linkage — fabricated steering shaft, mounting brackets, bearing, and tie rods.
- Brakes: hydraulic brake on the rear axle; throttle via an adapted bike brake lever, both controlled from the steering wheel.

## Results

Delivered a running go-kart built from a recycled shopping cart frame and off-the-shelf kart components, combining fabrication, welding, steering geometry, drivetrain alignment, and braking integration into one working vehicle. Also presented the build alongside physics calculations — velocity/acceleration, forces, friction, torque, and impact — on a project poster, including data from a custom-built speedometer.
